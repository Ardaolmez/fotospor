# Fotospor Turkish → English Glossary

Source of truth for translation. The skill re-reads this on every run and verifies that any Turkish term listed here is translated with the corresponding English term, accounting for inflected forms (Turkish is agglutinative — `sakatlık` may appear as `sakatlığı`, `sakatlandı`, `sakatlanan`).

**How to grow this file**: append a new row to the appropriate section's table. Keep the pipe `|` columns aligned roughly; the parser only requires that each data row starts with `|` and has three `|`-separated fields. If a Turkish term should remain in Turkish in the English output (e.g. club names), repeat the term in the English column and note `Keep Turkish spelling`.

## Translation rules (apply on every run)

- **Player names**: never translate. Keep verbatim, including diacritics.
- **Club names**: keep Turkish spelling with diacritics (`Beşiktaş`, `Fenerbahçe`, `Göztepe`).
- **Scores, dates, numbers, ages, jersey numbers**: preserve verbatim.
- **Headlines**: render natural English. Don't translate word-for-word; capture the meaning.
- **Sport-specific vocab**: a term may have different English in different sports — `mola` is "time-out" in volleyball/basketball but rendered "break" rarely in football. Use the section that matches the article's sport.
- **British football English**: for football articles, default to British football journalism conventions (`manager` not "head coach", `matchweek` not "gameday", `fixture` not "schedule", `side` or `club` for "team", `boots` not "cleats").
- **Glossary terms below**: must be translated per this table. If you find a better translation while working, add it as a new row instead of overriding silently.

## Rhetorical patterns and periphrastic epithets

### General rule for headlines and rhetorical phrases

Turkish headlines are rhetorical and often double-clauses. Render them as natural English you'd see in a sports section — preserve meaning, not structure. English sports prose is plainer than Turkish; trimming one flourish per paragraph usually reads better.
